Meta's Muse AI assistant raises privacy alarms, and Apple tightens its Mac settings in response

First reported 2026-10-02 · Latest update 2026-10-03

Most useful for: Coaches · Small Business

Wired reports that Meta's AI assistant Muse, which millions have downloaded, builds detailed profiles of users' friends and family, and that getting it to do useful work has privacy costs. Ars Technica reports that a tech columnist said Muse sent him a notification referencing a private message thread he says he never gave it permission to read. On Friday, Apple said it is changing its Mac privacy settings so third-party apps can't misuse broad file-access permissions to reach message histories. The details come from news reports, and we haven't seen Meta's side in these items.

Why it mattersIf you or your clients use an AI assistant that can see messages, calendars or files, it may see more than you intended, and client confidences could be exposed.
Try thisOpen the permissions or privacy settings on any AI assistant you use and switch off access to messages, email and files it doesn't truly need. Do this especially on any device where client conversations live.

Upsides

  • Apple's change shows platforms will respond when people complain
  • Assistants that can see your calendar and messages can save real admin time

Watch-outs

  • Permissions you grant once are easy to forget
  • Third parties' information (your clients' messages) is at stake, not just yours

Google is cutting free Gemini access to its lightest model from October 9

First reported 2026-10-06

Most useful for: Coaches · Small Business

According to The Verge, from October 9 free Gemini users will only get the entry-level Flash Lite model. The standard Flash model will need the $4.99-a-month AI Plus plan. That plan is also losing access to the top-tier Gemini Pro model. Pro and the advanced "Deep Think" reasoning option will be reserved for higher-priced plans.

Why it mattersIf you rely on free Gemini for drafting emails, session summaries or marketing copy, the quality and speed you're used to may change.
Try thisBefore October 9, note which of your regular Gemini tasks matter most. Then rerun one afterward and decide whether the free version is still good enough or worth paying for.

Upsides

  • Free access to a basic model continues
  • The entry paid plan is relatively cheap

Watch-outs

  • Free users lose the stronger models
  • Paid AI Plus subscribers also lose Pro access

AI job interviewers are becoming real: HackerRank says its tool has run over 500,000

First reported 2026-10-05

Most useful for: Coaches · Small Business

TechCrunch reports that HackerRank's AI interviewer has conducted more than 500,000 interviews, with Snowflake, Snorkel and Capgemini among early testers. The figure comes from the company. HackerRank mainly serves technical hiring, so this shows a direction rather than a widespread practice.

Why it mattersClients who are job hunting may soon face an AI interviewer, and small businesses will be pitched similar tools for screening candidates.
Try thisIf you coach job seekers, add a practice round on talking to an AI interviewer. If you hire, ask any screening vendor how candidates are judged and whether a person reviews the results.

Upsides

  • Could make first-round screening faster and more consistent
  • Candidates may get interviews they would otherwise miss

Watch-outs

  • Candidates may find it impersonal
  • Little independent evidence yet on fairness or accuracy
