Researchers warn that too much reliance on AI may wear down human judgment

First reported 2026-10-15

Most useful for: Coaches · Small Business

An MIT Sloan Management Review and Boston Consulting Group expert panel, now in its fifth year, says new research suggests that overusing or over-relying on AI may weaken people's thinking and moral judgment. The article uses the term "epistemic de-skilling," which means losing the ability to judge things for yourself. The panel's summary does not give sample sizes or methods, so these findings should be read as early and not yet settled.

Why it mattersCoaches and business owners are paid to make sound judgment calls, and a tool that quietly replaces that judgment could weaken the skill they sell.
Try thisPick one recurring decision in your business, such as hiring, pricing, or how you respond to a difficult client, and write down the exact step where a human makes the final call, not the AI.

Upsides

  • AI can speed up routine checks, which frees people to spend time on the hard decisions.
  • Guidance on where humans should stay in the loop can help teams use AI with more care.

Watch-outs

  • Heavy reliance may wear down the judgment needed to spot AI mistakes.
  • The research is new and the panel summary does not show how strong the evidence is.
Read the sources:sloanreview.mit.edu
