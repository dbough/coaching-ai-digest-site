Meta's new business terms may let it train AI on your linked website content

First reported 2026-10-16 · Latest update 2026-10-17

Most useful for: Coaches · Small Business

Meta is updating its commercial terms for business accounts on Facebook and Instagram. Legal experts quoted by Ars Technica and ZDNET say the wording would let Meta use content from websites linked from those accounts to train its AI, with no way to opt out, starting October 30. After backlash, Meta removed some of that wording, and a spokesperson said the change does not alter how it crawls the web. Reports still disagree about what the final terms allow, so the situation is not settled.

Why it mattersIf you run a business page on Meta or link to your own website from one, your content could end up in AI training without a clear choice to say no.
Try thisCheck the current commercial terms on your Meta business account this week, and check again after October 30. If the wording still covers your linked website and you object, ask Meta support in writing what applies to you.

Upsides

  • Meta says the update does not change how it crawls websites, and it removed the most criticized wording

Watch-outs

  • Legal experts say business accounts get no opt-out, so owners face a take-it-or-leave-it choice
  • Publishers and other site owners may lose control over content they have already published

OpenAI's own study shows how fast an AI tool can spread through a company

First reported 2026-10-20

Most useful for: Coaches · Small Business

A working paper from OpenAI's research team tracks how staff shifted to Codex, a tool built mainly for writing software. Its share of employees' AI work rose from under 10 percent in August 2025 to 99.8 percent by June 2026. Legal and recruiting teams went from almost no use to nearly 90 percent of their AI work in about five months, helped by company-wide training and regular feedback. Outside OpenAI, the researchers say adoption is lower and more uneven.

Why it mattersIt suggests that non-technical roles can adopt AI quickly when a business backs the change with training and feedback, which a small team can copy.
Try thisBefore rolling out any new AI tool to your team, plan a short training session and a check-in every two weeks for the first month.

Upsides

  • Shows non-technical teams, such as legal and recruiting, can adopt AI quickly with training and feedback
  • Gives concrete numbers on how a rollout can spread

Watch-outs

  • It is one company's internal data, written by an economist who works for that company
  • The tool is built for software work, and the paper says adoption elsewhere is slower and less even

Google Chat now posts AI meeting recaps with action items and owners

First reported 2026-10-19

Most useful for: Small Business

Google has added meeting recaps to Google Chat, part of Google Workspace. Each recap includes a short summary, key decisions, and action items assigned to named people, plus links to the full notes, recording, and transcript. This is a feature update from Google, not independent testing.

Why it mattersFor small teams already on Google Workspace, it could cut the time spent writing follow-up notes and tracking who does what.
Try thisIf your team uses Google Chat, try recaps after your next internal meeting, and before turning them on, check who can see them in your shared spaces.

Upsides

  • Less time spent writing follow-up notes and assigning tasks
  • Action items sit in one place with named owners

Watch-outs

  • Recaps spread meeting content to everyone in a shared space, which is a concern for private or client conversations
  • AI summaries can miss nuance, so check them before acting on them
