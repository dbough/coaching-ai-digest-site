How this works

The Human Side of AI is a short daily briefing on the AI developments that affect coaches and small businesses. It isn't general AI news. It covers what changes how you work, serve clients, market yourself, run your business, and protect the trust your clients place in you.

Please read. This briefing is selected and written automatically by AI, and no person reviews it before it's published. It can contain mistakes or miss nuance. Dan Bough and Completely Human Coaching are not responsible for the accuracy of this information. Always read the linked original sources and use your own judgment before acting on anything here.

1. Finding the news

Every morning an automated process checks a fixed list of about twenty sources for anything published since the previous issue. The list includes:

  • announcements from the major AI companies, such as OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft and Meta
  • product update blogs for everyday tools like Google Workspace and Microsoft 365
  • established technology journalism
  • business and management publications

That's usually somewhere between a few dozen and a hundred articles.

2. Choosing and summarizing

All of those articles go to one AI model (Anthropic's Claude) together, in a single pass. The model:

  • Groups coverage of the same event into one story, so you don't read the same news five times.
  • Keeps only what affects coaches and small businesses. A huge AI-industry headline gets left out if it doesn't change anything for you.
  • Rates each story by how much it matters to these readers, and whether it's most useful for coaches, small businesses, or both.
  • Writes a plain-language summary with why it matters, a small "Try this" suggestion, and links to the original sources it relied on.

If nothing clears the bar, the issue says so rather than filling space.

3. No human in the loop

No person selects, writes, edits, or fact-checks the stories. Dan Bough set up the automated process, and each issue is published automatically and isn't reviewed by a person first. That's why every story, "Worth watching" item, and suggestion links to its original source, and why you should read those sources and use your own judgment before acting on anything.

Spot a problem? Have a request?

If a summary gets something wrong, a story doesn't belong, something important was missed, or you'd like the briefing to work differently, please reach out to Dan Bough directly. Send a message on LinkedIn. Feedback helps improve which sources are used and how the AI chooses stories.
