Much of this week's AI news is about "agents": assistants that act for you across your apps instead of just answering questions. New study finds no sign yet that AI is cutting hiring of recent graduates

First reported 2026-09-25

Most useful for: Coaches · Small Business

A working paper from researchers Robert Fairlie and Jane Wu at Munich's CESifo says there is "no evidence of any significant, widespread displacement or reduction in hiring of recent college graduates." It contradicts a Stanford study reported last month, which found entry-level employment in AI-exposed jobs lagging other fields. A working paper hasn't been through formal peer review. The two studies use different approaches, so the question is still open.

Why it mattersMany coaching clients, and their kids, are anxious about AI and early-career jobs, and the evidence doesn't yet support a simple story either way.
Try thisWhen a client raises AI job fears, ask what they've actually seen in their own field before offering reassurance or alarm.
